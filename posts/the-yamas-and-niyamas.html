The Yamas & Niyamas

The practice beyond the mat, exploring life through the Yamas (ethical restraints) and Niyamas (self-observances).

The journey begins by looking inward. There, we discover the freedom to choose our attitude, our thoughts, and our actions—becoming skillful participants in the adventure of being alive.

What are Yamas and Niyamas?

Yamas = relationship with the external world; Niyamas = relationship with the inner world.

The Yamas and Niyamas are the first two limbs of Patanjali's Eight Limbs of Yoga. They form the ethical foundation of the yogic path, guiding us toward living with purpose, compassion, self-awareness, and inner peace.

Rather than a set of rules to follow, the Yamas and Niyamas are invitations to explore how we relate to ourselves, to others, and to the world around us. Each Sanskrit term carries layers of wisdom that unfold through study, self-reflection, and lived experience.

The Five Yamas

The Yamas are often described as ethical restraints or universal values. They guide how we interact with others and how we move through the world with awareness and compassion.

  • Ahimsa — Non-violence
    Kindness • Compassion • Self-Love
    Reflection: How can I bring more kindness and compassion into my thoughts, words, and actions?

  • Satya — Truthfulness
    Honesty • Authenticity • Integrity
    Reflection: Am I being honest with myself and others?

  • Asteya — Non-stealing
    Contentment • Respect • Generosity
    Reflection: What am I taking that is not truly mine — time, energy, attention, or resources?

  • Brahmacharya — Non-excess (Moderation)
    Balance • Self-control • Mindfulness
    Reflection: Where can I create more balance and use my energy more consciously?

  • Aparigraha — Non-possessiveness
    Letting go • Simplicity • Gratitude
    Reflection: What can I let go of to create more space and freedom within myself?
>

The Five Niyamas

The Niyamas are often described as personal observances or inner disciplines. They guide how we relate to ourselves, encouraging self-awareness, growth, and a deeper connection with our inner world.

  • Saucha – Purity
    Clarity • Simplicity • Presence
    Reflection: What can I cleanse or let go of to create more clarity in my life?

  • Santosha – Contentment
    Gratitude • Acceptance • Joy
    Reflection: Can I appreciate this moment just as it is?

  • Tapas – Discipline
    Commitment • Consistency • Growth
    Reflection: What small practice can I commit to today that supports my growth?

  • Svadhyaya – Self-study
    Curiosity • Awareness • Reflection
    Reflection: What is this moment teaching me about myself?

  • Ishvara Pranidhana – Surrender
    Trust • Faith • Letting go
    Reflection: What am I holding onto that I can gently surrender?


More Than a Definition

Everything within us naturally finds its way to express itself outward. By tending to our inner world, we shape how we show up in the outer world.

As Etty Hillesum wrote in her diary, "Ultimately, we have just one moral duty: to reclaim large areas of peace in ourselves, more and more peace, and to reflect it toward others." Through the practice of the Yamas and Niyamas, we cultivate that inner peace within ourselves, allowing it to naturally extend into our relationships, communities, and the world around us.

Reference
