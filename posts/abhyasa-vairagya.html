Vairagya (Non-attachment) & Abhyasa (Practice): The Ease in Effort

Yoga Sutra 1.12

abhyasa vairagyabhyam tannirodhah

“TThe fluctuations of the mind are brought toward stillness through practice (abhyasa) and non-attachment (vairagya).”

Abhyasa and Vairagya - The two complementary practices

– Abhyasa — sustained practice; the willingness to return again and again to what is steady, clear, and worth cultivating.;
– Vairagya — non-attachment; the willingness to loosen our grasping toward what we want, what we dislike, what we expect, and even toward the results of our practice..

Why do we practice abhyasa and vairagya

Someone praises us → we feel happy.
Someone criticises us → we feel hurt or angry.
Something we hoped for happens → excitement.
Something doesn't happen → disappointment.

The experience happens. Then the mind interprets, reacts, remembers, anticipates, compares, and attaches meaning to it. A pleasant experience can become something we cling to; an unpleasant one can become something we resist.

The ease in effort:
– abhyasa: I keep showing up;
– vairagya: I don't need to control what comes from showing up.

We are grateful that we can feel. We experience joy, sadness, anger, disappointment, excitement. These movements are part of being alive.

This is where we practice abhyasa and vairagya.

What makes abhyasa a good practice?

Yoga Sutra 1.14

sa tu dirgha kala nairantarya satkarasevito drdhabhumih

dirgha kala · nairantarya · satkarasevito · drdhabhumih

Practice becomes firmly grounded when:

  • dirgha kala – Long time
    Application: Can I trust that practice unfolds over time, rather than expecting immediate results?
  • nairantarya – Without interruption
    Application: Can I keep returning to the practice, even after days when I lose my rhythm?
  • satkara – With sincerity and devotion
    Application: Can I approach my practice with care, respect, and wholehearted presence rather than treating it as another task to complete?
  • drdha bhumi – Firmly grounded
    Application: Can I allow practice to become a steady foundation that supports my life, rather than something dependent on my mood?

Abhyasa is the practice of returning — to stay present rather than being carried away by the movements of the mind. Vairagya balances it by creating a little space between experience, that's the space we choose direction without forcing the outcomes

Vairagya - the friend of abhyasa

We begin with an intention and a goal. But somewhere along the way, we may become so focused on reaching it that we lose sight of why we began.

Vairagya can be practiced in the way we relate to ourselves and to our experiences. We notice what we are holding onto, and practice meeting it with honesty, kindness, and compassion.

  • Practice – Returning again and again
    Application: Can I notice when I become attached and gently practice loosening my grip?
  • Honesty – Seeing clearly
    Application: Can I honestly acknowledge what I want, resist, fear, or cling to without judging myself for it?
  • Kindness – Meeting myself gently
    Application: Can I meet my own reactions with kindness rather than making detachment another thing I have to be good at?
  • Compassion – Allowing myself to be human
    Application: Can I give myself space when things do not go as I hoped, without forcing myself to let go before I am ready?
  • Letting Go – Loosening the grip
    Application: Can I release my need to control the experience or its outcome?

Calm While Being Fully Involved

Life is a series of experiences. The practice is always with us, on and off the mat.
Abhyasa is the willingness to return. Vairagya creates the space to choose.

As you notice the waves and ripples in the mind, there's no need to stop the waves or force the water to be still.

Simply notice them, and remember that beneath the movement, there is still water.
